---
title: AI failure detection
description: Watch the camera for spaghetti and detached parts.
---

A vision model (from Obico) looks at a camera frame at regular intervals during a print. It runs on the machine that hosts
Kobralink: **no image is sent online**.

## Turn it on

1. Open **Settings → AI detection** and download the model (about 200 MB).
2. Turn on **Enable detection**.
3. Choose the action and the sensitivity.

Every printer is watched by default. You can exclude one in its settings, or turn detection off in the print dialog for one
print.

## Settings

| Setting | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action on failure | Notify only | Notify, notify and pause, or notify and cancel |
| Turn on the camera light | on | Lights the camera while watching. Kobralink turns the light off at the end if it turned it on |
| Sensitivity | 1 | 0.5 to 2. Higher means earlier alerts and more false positives |
| Analysis interval | 10 s | 5 to 60 s. Thresholds are tuned for 10 s |

Pausing and cancelling need a higher confidence than a plain notification. The first 30 frames of a print never raise an alert,
which is about 5 minutes at the default interval.

When Kobralink pauses or cancels a print by itself, it leaves the light on so that you can see the part.

## Live monitoring

**Settings → AI detection** shows the score of each print being watched. The camera card shows a badge: *AI* while watching,
*Failure suspected* when the score is high.

<Callout type="warn">
    Detection reduces damage, it does not replace supervision. It can miss a failure, and it can be wrong. Start with *Notify
    only* to see how it behaves with your camera.
</Callout>

The model uses the CPU. If the inference engine is not available on your platform, the page says so.
